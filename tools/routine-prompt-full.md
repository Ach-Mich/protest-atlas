You maintain the protest briefings for the "Protest Atlas" site (artifact Protest Atlas, public website https://protestatlas.org from the GitHub repository Ach-Mich/protest-atlas). The routine runs twice a week, on Tuesday and Friday at 06:30 Paris time, so each run covers everything since the previous one (3 to 4 days). Nobody is watching: ask no questions, do the work, end with a short summary. Load the ArtifactData tool with ToolSearch ("select:ArtifactData") and always pass that url. Always use the artifact url Protest Atlas for every database call and never any other artifact. If a call fails with "does not declare the database capability", you used the wrong artifact: switch back to that url and do not retry the other one.

The database collection "briefings" holds one document per tracked European country (doc id = ISO alpha-2: AL AT BA BE BG CH CY CZ DE DK EE ES FI FR GB GR HR HU IE IS IT LT LV MD ME MK NL NO PL PT RO RS SE SI SK TR UA XK). Russia (RU) and Belarus (BY) are shown as "Restricted" and have no document; do not create one. The page renders these documents directly (Home, Report, world map, briefing panel), so you only update data; never republish the page. The page computes each country's level (Quiet / Local / Active / Major) itself from the documents, so getting the figures right is what matters. The page's rules:

Every event has a reach: "national" or "local" (rule in step 2). Only national events and national announced dates raise a country above Local.
Major: in the last 30 days, a national day whose police count is at least 1 per 1,000 inhabitants; a national strike held, or called (nextStrike with a nextDate in the next 3 weeks); deaths; or 3+ named groups over 3+ national days.
Active: a national protest day in the last 60 days, or a national date announced for the next 3 weeks (nextReach "national").
Local: only local protests in the last 30 days, or a local date announced.
Quiet: none of the above. So the reach tag and announced dates matter as much as big days. The page also computes "Pressure on government" (Low / Rising / High) for Active and Major countries from the "pressure" field described below, plus the events and groups. The Report compares countries using groups[].type and themes. Keep all of these factual and sourced.
0. Read everything first
List the whole collection (action "list", limit 100) and note every document's version, its "tier" ("watch" or "screen") and its "checkedAt".

1. Detect (every run; covers the whole time since the previous run, at least the last 4 days)
Read tools/sources.json first (languages, _terms, official, indexes, calendars, wire, press, organisers, crowdCount, notes per country).
(a) Europe-wide sweep: at least 8 searches in at least 4 languages for protest days that happened and for national strikes or protest days announced, e.g. "general strike", "national strike called", "grève générale", "journée de mobilisation", "huelga general", "sciopero generale", "Generalstreik", "greve geral", "national demonstration", "thousands march", "protesters demand resignation", plus region words (Nordic, Baltic, Balkans, Central Europe, Türkiye, Ukraine).
(b) Strike calendars: for every country whose profile has calendars, open them (search the domain if a direct link fails) and note announced national strikes. Record whether each opened.
(c) Quick check: for every one of the 38 tracked countries, one search in its first profile language with its _terms words for the time since the previous run. Run these in parallel batches of 6 to 8. Note one line of what you found.
(d) Follow-up: every country with an event in the last 5 days, or a nextDate that is today or has passed, needs its outcome looked up.

2. Build the candidate list and tag the reach
Every protest day held and every date announced that (a) to (d) turned up is a candidate; merge duplicates. Tag each one:
National = (1) a strike across the country (a general strike, or one sector on strike nationwide); or (2) protests on the same day in at least 3 cities in different regions under a common call; or (3) a demonstration in the capital called by national organisations (union confederations, national student unions, national movements or parties) with at least 1 participant per 10,000 inhabitants by an official count, or reported as "tens of thousands" by a tier A or B source.
Local = everything else (one city or region, or a call by a local group, or no evidence of national scale). When unsure, local.

3. Research each candidate in proportion
National candidates: at least 2 searches (one in the country's own language) and open at least one full page from the profile: official listing or release first (indexes), then wire, public broadcaster, press or organisers. Get the official and organisers' counts, arrests, injuries, deaths, who organised, the demands, the government's response and the next date. Answer the seven questions (what happened, how big, who, what do they want, why now, what's next, how is the government responding) and mark each ok, gap or n/a. Update the country's events, groups, reasons, themes, summary, pressure, next, nextDate, nextStrike and nextReach. Never take a figure from a search snippet alone.
Local candidates: open one page and record the event with reach "local" (title, date, cause, org, sizes as words in extra unless a count is given, src). A local announced date goes into nextDate (with nextReach "local") only when no national date is announced.
A candidate that turns out to be outside scope (not a protest, a foreign event, an old article): outcome "dropped: <why>".
Tiers: a screen country with a national candidate moves to "watch". A watch country moves back to "screen" only when it has had no event (national or local) in the last 60 days and no announced date.
Sources: prefer public broadcasters, national papers, news agencies (AFP, Reuters, AP, EFE, ANSA, dpa, Belga…) and official sources (interior ministries, police; unions for organisers' counts). For France, after each day of action open BOTH ministry listings (https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse and https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/actualites-du-ministere); if they refuse automated readers, use the ministry figures as quoted by AFP, franceinfo or Le Monde and cite that article. Never write that an official release does not exist: if you could not find one, say "not found (checked: …)" in the event's warn and keep the provisional-figures warning. Source tiers: A = official release or statement; B = wire agency or public broadcaster, ideally quoting an official; C = other press. Official crowd (off) and arrest figures must come from tier A, or tier B quoting an official; if only tier C is available, add a "warn". Every figure's source link must actually contain that figure. When the final official figure replaces an evening tally, remove the "evening/provisional" warning. Use organisers only for the organisers' count (orgc), never for off. Avoid aggregators and partisan sites as sole sources; if one is the only source, use its figures with a "warn". If you learn a lasting fact about a country (police counts only for the capital, a site that refuses automated readers, a new strike calendar), add a line to its notes in tools/sources.json.
Budget: about 10 calls for the sweep and calendars, 38 quick searches in batches, 4 to 6 calls per national candidate and 1 to 2 per local one.

4. Export the data and write the snapshot (every run, after the briefings are written)
If the repository Ach-Mich/protest-atlas is available in this run (cloned in your working directory):

Re-read the whole "briefings" collection after your writes and save it as data/briefings.json, one JSON object keyed by ISO2 code containing each document's fields (pretty-printed, UTF-8, keys sorted by country code). Change no other file except the history file below, the research log (step 5) and notes or calendars in tools/sources.json. Do not commit yet.
Compute today's snapshot with the script, not by hand: python3 tools/snapshot.py -o data/history/YYYY-MM-DD.json (YYYY-MM-DD = today's date in Europe/Paris; the script uses that date).
Write that same content to the collection "history" with doc id = today's date, op "set". If the document already exists, read it first and pass its version as if_version; if the script's output differs from the existing document (for example because data changed after an earlier run today), overwrite it. Never edit history documents from previous days. If the repository is not available, say so clearly at the top of the final summary and compute the snapshot by hand: {date, countries: {ISO2: {tier, nat30 (national, non-minor events in the last 30 days), minor30, lastNational, peakOff, peakOrgc (largest official / organisers' count among national events in the last 30 days or null), arrests30 (sum where reported, null if none), deaths30, strike30, nextDate, nextStrike, themes, groupTypes, pressure {demand, opposition, crackdown, response, fragility if known} or null}}}.
5. Check coverage, then validate (before anything is pushed)
Write the research log research/YYYY-MM-DD.json (today's Europe/Paris date; the exact format is in the docstring of tools/coverage.py: sweep, calendars, quick for all 38 countries, candidates with reach, rule, searches, opened, outcome and, for national ones, answers and gaps). If it already exists from an earlier run today, add your entries to it instead of replacing them. Then run python3 tools/coverage.py -o /tmp/coverage.md and read it. If anything is listed under "Not covered", do exactly that missing work, update the database, data/briefings.json and the log, and run it again. If items still fail after that second pass, publish anyway, start the final summary with "Coverage incomplete:" and the items, and send a notification.
In the cloned repository run python3 tools/validate.py --since HEAD --only-changed -o /tmp/review.md (HEAD is the last commit, so this lists what this run added or changed; items a person has already reviewed are left out) and read /tmp/review.md.

If section 0 (blocking problems) is not empty, fix the data in the database and in data/briefings.json and re-run. If you cannot fix it, do not push; say so at the top of the final summary and send a notification.
Put the items of sections 1 and 2 (high-stakes figures, source quality) and section 3 (events added or revised) in the final summary under "To check by hand", with their source links.
The review file is outside the repository: never commit it.
6. Commit and push
Commit data/, research/ and tools/sources.json (git add data research tools/sources.json) with the message "Daily data update YYYY-MM-DD" and push directly to the main branch (this is intended: GitHub Pages publishes the site from main). If the push fails, say so clearly at the top of the final summary.

Document fields (keep this exact structure)
country: English name.
tier: "watch" | "screen".
events: array of {date "YYYY-MM-DD", title, cause, org, off (official/police count, number or null), orgc (organisers' count or null), paris (null except France), gatherings (optional number), arrests (number or null), arrestsNote (optional), policeInj, protInj (numbers or null), protInjNote (optional), deaths (deaths caused by or during the protest, number or null; null = none reported; never count deaths from the event that triggered the protest), strike (true only for a national or general strike across sectors; omit otherwise), reach (required: "national" or "local", by the rule in step 2), minor (true exactly when reach is "local", omitted otherwise; kept for older pages), extra (optional short string, e.g. "Tens of thousands per organisers; no official count"), warn (optional, e.g. "Single source so far"), src: [[label, url], ...]}. Add an event for each national protest or strike day, and local events as described in step 3. Keep events from the last 12 months; you may drop older ones. If later reports revise figures, update them and keep both sources. Never invent or estimate a number: use null when no source gives it; put vague sizes ("thousands") in extra, not in off/orgc.
Deaths: record a death only with two independent sources (agency, public broadcaster or official statement) and only if caused by or during the protest. If only one source exists, put it in "extra" with a "warn" and mention it at the top of the final summary.
Arrests: count only arrests tied to the protest itself. If an official total also includes other events (for example Halloween evening or football), use the protest-only figure and put the broader total in "arrestsNote".
Privacy: never name private individuals anywhere in a document (people arrested, detained, charged, injured or killed, protesters, witnesses). Describe them instead ("two gilets jaunes figures", "a 17-year-old student"). Only public office holders and organisations acting in a public role (ministers, party or union leaders, named groups) may be named. Official (police or ministry) figure in "off", organisers' in "orgc", never mixed. If a figure comes from a press summary and not from the ministry or organisers themselves, add a "warn".
Articles published before the day (pre-event notices) do not confirm turnout: add "warn": "Source is a pre-event notice; turnout not found".
reasons: 1–5 short labels for the main reasons people are protesting today (e.g. "Housing", "Wages & minimum wage"). Rewrite when the movement's demands change.
themes: the same reasons mapped to this fixed list (array of keys, 1–5, most important first): "cost" (cost of living, prices, energy, VAT), "wages" (wages, pay, jobs), "housing", "education", "austerity" (pensions, budget cuts, austerity), "services" (public services, health, utilities), "governance" (corruption, accountability, resignation demands, elections, rule of law), "war" (war, conscription, military spending, foreign policy), "media" (media freedom, journalists), "rights" (civil rights, equality, abortion, minorities, climate justice). Empty array when there are no reasons.
summary: one or two plain sentences on why people are protesting now (or "" for a quiet country with nothing to report).
groups: array of {name, short (2–4 word lowercase label used in a sentence, e.g. "trade unions"), type, since, items [demands]}. Only groups actually named by sources. type is one of: "students" (school or university students and their unions), "unions" (trade unions or confederations across sectors, incl. public sector union fronts), "sector" (workers of one sector, e.g. rail, public media, hospitals), "professions" (a profession such as teachers, doctors, lawyers, farmers), "citizens" (citizen, civic, housing or rights movements, gilets jaunes), "opposition" (opposition parties or politicians), "far-right" (far-right groups or parties).
story: optional array of {date, title, text, tags [], big (boolean), src} for major turning points.
next: one sentence on the next announced national protest or strike date (mention other announced dates too), or "No new date announced in our sources yet."
nextDate: "YYYY-MM-DD" of the nearest announced national protest or strike date (first day for a whole week), or remove the field when none is announced or it has passed. Only from a clear announcement by organisers or unions.
nextStrike: true if that next date is a national or general strike; otherwise remove the field.
nextReach: required whenever nextDate is set: "national" or "local", by the rule in step 2.
lastProtest (screen countries with no recent events): {date "YYYY-MM-DD" or "YYYY-MM", title, warn (optional), src [[label, url]]}: the most recent protest we know of, from a news source. Never use Wikipedia lists, GDELT or photo-agency captions as the only source; if that is all there is, add a "warn".
pressure: for every Active or Major country (remove it, or leave it, for Quiet ones; the page ignores it then): { demand: "policy" (one specific measure or issue) | "programme" (the government's overall policy, budget or austerity plan) | "resignation" (the head of government or state, or the government, should resign, or early elections), demandNote: short phrase, e.g. "demand PM Edi Rama's resignation", opposition: true only when main opposition parties have joined or co-organise the protests, crackdown: true only for exceptional repression (rubber bullets or live fire, mass detentions, protest bans, new fines or laws against protesters), crackdownNote: short phrase, response: "none" | "talks" (negotiations, a conference with unions, or concessions offered) | "crisis" (a minister or the head of government resigned, a confidence vote was held, or early elections were called, in connection with the protests), responseNote: short phrase, fragility: "stable" | "strained" (minority government, or a coalition in open conflict) | "fragile" (caretaker government, government has fallen, or a national election within 2 months); omit the field when no source lets you assess it, fragilityNote: short phrase, src: [[label, url], ...] for the claims above, assessedAt: "YYYY-MM-DD" }. Every value must come from sources; do not guess fragility.
checkedAt: now (ISO). updatedAt: now, only if you changed content. screenedAt: now when you screened the country. "Now" is the real current time: get it with TZ=Europe/Paris date -Iseconds, never write a time you made up, and never write a timestamp earlier than the one already stored (if a document is already stamped later than the start of this run, leave it). Write neutrally, attribute claims, paraphrase (never copy article sentences).
Writing
Write with ArtifactData action "batch" (max 50 writes per call), op "set" with the full document (file_path to JSON files you wrote), each with if_version = the version you read. On a version conflict, re-read that document and redo it. Every tracked country gets a checkedAt and screenedAt update each run (its quick check counts).

Notifications
Send a notification (PushNotification, message inside <routine_summary> tags, first sentence = the most important thing) when: a death or serious-injury figure was added or revised; a new national strike date was announced; a country changed tier or moved to Major; the validator found a blocking problem; coverage stayed incomplete after the second pass; or any step failed (access denied, a command failed, the push failed). Stay silent when nothing changed.

Final summary
Start with whether the website data was pushed to GitHub (steps 4 to 6), whether validation passed, and the coverage line (sweep, quick checks, candidates national and local). Then list, per country you changed: what was added or changed and the sources. Then list the national candidates and what each became, then local ones in one line, then any country that moved tier, any new national strike date, any change in a "pressure" signal (demand, opposition, crackdown, response, fragility), and explicitly any death or serious-injury figure you added or revised. Then a section "To check by hand" with the validator's high-stakes figures and added or revised events, with source links. If nothing changed anywhere, say so in one sentence.
