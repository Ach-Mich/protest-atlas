France

Last checked Tuesday 24 November at 22:29.

Began in September as a high-school protest over teacher shortages and run-down schools, widened to the cost of living and, on 5 November, to wages and public services with the unions. Public-service unions, joined by the CGT and youth groups, struck again on 17 November for general pay rises, with 146,000 marchers by the official count, down from 575,000 on 5 November.

Why they protest: Education funding & teacher shortages; Cost of living; Wages & minimum wage; Public services; Tax fairness.

Next protest, Tuesday 1 December. Eight public-service unions call a national strike and day of action on Tuesday 1 December over pay, and the CGT, CFDT and UNSA invite private-sector workers to join; the government meets the unions again on 11 December. The Interfac Île-de-France student assembly calls a mobilisation on 26 November.

Who is protesting, and what they want

High-school & university students Since September
Replace absent teachers · €10bn for schools and universities · Renovate school buildings · Repeal Parcoursup

Gilets jaunes & citizens Since 17 October
Lower cost of living · Purchasing power · Fairer society · Freeze energy and fuel prices

Trade unions Since 5 November
Raise the minimum wage (no boost since 2012) · Higher pay for all · Tax fairness · Protect public services · €100 net more per month (CFDT)

Pressure on government

Demand
Change of programme. Wages, minimum wage and public services against the budget
Response
Talks offered. PM held a social conference on 9 November (minimum-wage rise from 1 December) and meets unions again on 11 December, but no index-point rise
Government
Strained. Minority government that has had to survive censure motions

Sources: Echos Plus, 6 Nov 2026, Euronews, 6 Jul 2026, Wikipedia: Second Lecornu government, France 24, 9 Nov 2026, Sortiraparis, 10 Nov 2026, Euronews, 17 Nov 2026, Brut, 20 Nov 2026

Protest days

Public-sector strike day with marches across France

Tuesday 17 November. 146,000 official · 500,000 organisers

Paris 28,000 per the Paris police (175,000 per unions). Toulouse over 20,000 and Lyon 7,000 per police (Lyon 14,000 per unions); Marseille about 5,000 and Rennes 4,500 per prefectures. End-of-day strike rates: 2.79% in the state civil service, 2.52% territorial, 2.60% hospitals; 3.76% of teachers. Turnout down from 575,000 on 5 November. Scuffles in some cities; water cannon used in Rennes.

Sources: Euronews, 17 Nov 2026, Al Jazeera / AFP, 17 Nov 2026, Sud Radio, 17 Nov 2026

Gilets jaunes "Acte V" and youth marches

Saturday 14 November. 106,000 official · 200,000 organisers · 219 arrests

Turnout down from 7 November. Incidents in Marseille, Grenoble, Lyon, Bordeaux and Poitiers per the ministry; Nantes accounted for 52 arrests (prefecture). Unions did not file routes.

Sources: franceinfo live, 14 Nov 2026, Ouest-France live, 14 Nov 2026

Education strike day: students, high-school pupils and teachers march

Tuesday 10 November. Turnout not reported

Education ministry: 49 of about 3,700 lycées blocked at 09:30 and 1.55% of education staff on strike at midday. Toulouse 3,500 per the prefecture. The interprofessional intersyndicale did not call a march. A regional councillor was splashed with petrol outside a blocked lycée in Chelles; an investigation was opened and the President condemned the attack.

Sources: franceinfo live, 10 Nov 2026, Sortiraparis, 10 Nov 2026

Gilets jaunes "Acte IV" and youth march

Saturday 7 November. 146,000 official · 250,000 organisers · 360 arrests

Main union confederations did not officially take part. Mostly calm; clashes at the end of the Paris march at Nation and in Lyon; banned rally held at Concorde.

Sources: Quartz FR / AFP, 7 Nov 2026, Sud Radio live, 7 Nov 2026, Interior Ministry release (provisional 18:00 tally), 7 Nov 2026

Interprofessional strike and demonstrations

Thursday 5 November. 575,000 official · 1,600,000 organisers · 448 arrests

15.8% of teachers and 9.9% of state civil servants on strike; about 60 of 184 universities disrupted

Sources: Interior Ministry release, 5 Nov 2026, L'essentiel, 5 Nov 2026, Sud Radio, 6 Nov 2026, France 24 live (Interior Ministry 18:00 tally), 5 Nov 2026

"Convergence des colères": youth, gilets jaunes and unions join

Saturday 17 October. 550,000 official · 800,000 organisers · 2,628 arrests

Looting and fires in Paris, Lyon, Limoges and Marseille; 1 death: a man died of cardio-respiratory arrest on Boulevard Magenta (Paris) as the march passed, per the Paris police prefecture; cause not specified

Sources: Paris Match Belgique, 18 Oct 2026, RTBF, 17 Oct 2026, i24NEWS: man dies of cardio-respiratory arrest during the Paris march, Le Tribunal du Net: demonstrator dies on Boulevard Magenta, Interior Ministry, 31 Oct 2026 release (arrests on 17 Oct)

National day of high-school and student demonstrations ("Acte III")

Tuesday 6 October. 256,000 official · 450,000 organisers · 488 arrests

More than 80 cities. Blockades at several Paris lycées (Hélène-Boucher, Voltaire, Louis-le-Grand, Henry IV) and at the Sorbonne and ENS Paris-Saclay. Education Ministry: 891 establishments (250 in Île-de-France) had classes suspended, fully or partly, all causes combined

Sources: Euronews (FR), 6 Oct 2026: 891 lycées perturbés (ministère), Paris Match Belgique, 6 Oct 2026

High-school blockades: about 400 lycées closed

Friday 2 October. Turnout not reported

Nearly 2,000 arrests by 1 Oct (Interior Ministry); unions received by the education minister; class suspensions expected in 400–500 lycées on 5 Oct

Sources: Euronews, 2 Oct 2026, DH, 2 Oct 2026

Public-service strike and demonstrations over pay (first joint call by all eight public-sector unions since 2018)

Tuesday 29 September. 206,000 official · 300,000 organisers · 440 arrests

About 170 gatherings; strike rate 9.64% in the state civil service at midday (ministry); public sector only, not a general strike. In parallel, high-school pupils took action: 338 schools still affected at the end of the day, 45 fully blocked

Sources: BFMTV: CGT claims over 300,000 demonstrators, 70,000 in Paris, BFMTV live blog, 29 Sep 2026 (ministry figure, schools, arrests), Acteurs publics (strike rates)

High-school blockades start in Créteil and spread across Île-de-France

Monday 21 September. Turnout not reported

Sources differ on the exact start date (18 to 21 Sep); the Créteil education authority counted a dozen new blockades on 25 Sep

Sources: Euronews, 30 Sep 2026, Euronews, 29 Sep 2026
