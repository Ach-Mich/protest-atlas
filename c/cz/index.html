Czechia

Last checked Tuesday 24 November at 22:29.

Organisers said about 400,000 people rallied on Letná plain in Prague on 15 November, the largest protest yet against the Babiš government's plan to scrap licence fees and fund Czech Television and Radio from the state budget; public media staff had struck on 3 November.

Why they protest: Public media independence; Government accountability.

Next protest, Wednesday 2 December. Milion chvilek calls a nationwide "warning" action on Wednesday 2 December at 16:36 (signs in windows) and invites the cabinet to a public debate in Prague that evening.

Who is protesting, and what they want

Public media staff, backed by unions and university rectors October
Withdraw the public media funding bill · Keep licence-fee funding

Milion chvilek pro demokracii Since 25 October
Withdraw the public media funding bill · Remove Tomio Okamura as Chamber speaker · Take the defence ministry away from the SPD · Public debate with the cabinet

Pressure on government

Demand
Policy change. Withdraw the public media funding bill
Response
No response yet. PM Babiš and coalition leaders say the licence fee will still be abolished

Sources: ČT24, 15 Nov 2026

Protest days

Milion chvilek rally on Letná plain, Prague

Sunday 15 November. 400,000 organisers

Organisers estimated 350,000 to 450,000 (about 400,000); police do not publish counts. Emergency services treated 27 people and took two to hospital, mainly for collapses and minor injuries. Police reported a few minor conflicts and seized four drones.

Sources: ČT24, 15 Nov 2026, Deník N live, 15 Nov 2026

Public media strike

Tuesday 3 November. Turnout not reported

Full-day strike by ČT and ČRo staff; thousands rallied on Wenceslas Square (initiative "Veřejnoprávně") and up to 10,000 in Brno, per estimates.

Sources: Al Jazeera (announcement), 25 Oct 2026, Seznam Zprávy, 4 Nov 2026
